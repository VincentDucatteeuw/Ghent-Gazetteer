Ghent city directories

The Wegwijzer der stad Gent is a yearly address book. Each volume lists the same inhabitants in several lists: the alphabetical list (name, occupation, address), the trade directory (name and address under each heading, such as Kruideniers, grocers), the list per street (the residents of each street by house number, from 1895) and the street list (the street names, with French name, location, police district and canton). This site holds the volumes of 1878, 1880, 1882, 1895, 1896 (the trade directory only) and 1898. The scans are from Ghent University Library; the text was read automatically (OCR) and split into fields.

What is where

  • Directory entries: every line read from the alphabetical list, the trade directory and the list per street, in the order of the book. Choose a volume and a list to read one book. Each line shows the text as read, its page and, where the library has the scans online (1878, 1895 and 1896), a crop of the scan.
  • People and firms: those followed through the volumes, with all their entries.
  • Streets through the years: one street through the years. A street's page lists all its entries (who lived there) and its lines in the street list (French name, location, police district, canton).
  • Occupations on the map: see below.

The filter Street (latest) on people and firms is the last known street. Everyone who ever lived in a street is on the street's page, or in the directory entries (filter on volume and street).

People and firms through the years

A person or firm is a person, household or firm followed through the volumes. Within a volume, the lines of the different lists are linked automatically on name, initial, street, house number and occupation (Splink, match probability ≥ 0.9). Then each volume is linked to the next, also when someone moved; someone missing from a volume is looked for in the volume after it. Another initial (a son), or a firm and a person, are never merged. A widow who continues her husband's business or household succeeds him (Widow succeeds: ja).

  • Match probability across the years: the weakest link between two volumes.
  • Kind: organisatie for firms, brothers, sisters and institutions; otherwise persoon.

Streets and occupations

A street is one street through the years: the street lists of all volumes are put together, with spelling and OCR variants and the list's own cross-references (ziet ...). The names per year are shown with the street. A street found in no street list says In a street list: nee.

Occupations are coded in HISCO (automatically, with OccCANINE) and grouped into major groups. The occupation as printed is shown next to it. In the trade directory the occupation is the heading the name is listed under.

Occupations on the map

The map shows, for one volume and one occupation (HISCO), how many persons and firms had that occupation in each street. Someone listed in more than one list (alphabetical, per street, trade directory) counts once. Streets are drawn as they run today: every street of the directories is matched to a street of today, first through the Ghent Gazetteer's hand-made links (renamed streets too), otherwise by name and postcode. A street that cannot be matched with certainty (gone, renamed, or more than one candidate) is not on the map; how many persons that concerns is shown above the map. Grey: residents that year, none with this occupation; dashed grey: no entries that year.

Sources: Straten Gent (City of Ghent, data.stad.gent, Modellicentie Gratis Hergebruik); basemap © OpenStreetMap contributors, Protomaps.

Limitations

  • OCR makes mistakes, especially in names; when in doubt, compare with the crop or the scan (📖).
  • Links, HISCO codes and streets are automatic and can be wrong, especially over a long gap (1882–1895) and for renamed streets.
  • For 1880, 1882 and 1898 no scans are linked (yet).
